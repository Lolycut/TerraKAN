## Abstract

In this paper, I introduce **TerraKAN (`Frac-ML-KAT-MoE`)**, a neuro-symbolic Scientific Machine Learning (SciML) architecture designed to solve the ill-posed inverse problem of soil bioindication and long-horizon self-purification forecasting from sparse ($6\text{--}8$ time points), irregular (up to 50% missingness), and noisy (25–40% multiplicative error) enzymatic and respiratory measurements without direct chemical assays of the contaminant. Whereas polynomial Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (`ChebPIKAN`) suffer from catastrophic Runge-type divergence outside the observation window ($R^2 = -8.13$ on $t \in [150, 365]\text{ days}$) and classical Bayesian ODE solvers (`MAP-ODE` with a Soil Atlas prior) fail to reconstruct unmeasured contaminant loads ($R^2 = -0.191$) due to neglecting non-Markovian micropore trapping ($\alpha < 1$), I unify four mathematical components into an end-to-end differentiable framework: (1) a causal Fractional Kolmogorov-Arnold Transformer encoder (`Frac-KAT`) with a singular Abel–Riemann–Liouville memory kernel $K(t,s) \propto (t-s)^{\alpha-1}/\Gamma(\alpha)$ and strict weight tying of the fractional order $\alpha$; (2) a pole-free global `[3/4]` Padé Mittag-Leffler spectral basis (`MittagLefflerPade34`) that matches exact asymptotic laws as $z \to 0$ and $z \to \infty$ with denominator $Q_4(z) \ge 1.001$; (3) a log-exponential `MultKAN` module that discovers Shelford pH tolerance bells and power-law environmental synergies alongside a sparse Top-2 biokinetic Mixture-of-Experts (`PhysicsExpertBank`) separating Haldane substrate inhibition from Monod nutrient limitation; and (4) a dynamic memory-aware soil health index $\text{ИБСП}_\alpha(t)$. Across 500 held-out test scenarios on $t \in [150, 365]\text{ days}$, TerraKAN achieves $R^2 = 0.908 \pm 0.019$ ($\text{RMSE} = 0.181\,\text{EC}_{50}$) on the unmeasured contaminant load $\tilde{P}(t)$ and $R^2 = 0.886 \pm 0.016$ ($\text{RMSE} = 6.88\%$) on $\text{ИБСП}_\alpha(t)$ at $0.50\text{ ms}$ per field ($10.1\times$ faster than `MAP-ODE`). I further validate the frozen checkpoint (`terrakan_v4_frozen.pt`) on a 36-vessel microcosm protocol across sandy, loamy, and peat soil series, demonstrating 100% separation between healthy controls, nutrient-depleted clean soils, and contaminated regimes without control-flag leakage.

## 1. Introduction & Gap Analysis

Soil ecosystems subjected to xenobiotic, hydrocarbon, or heavy-metal stress exhibit pronounced non-Markovian memory governed by anomalous subdiffusion and sorption-desorption trapping inside hierarchical organo-mineral micropores ([Yan et al., 2026](#references)). Consequently, the relaxation of bioavailable contaminant load $\tilde{P}(t)$ follows heavy-tailed power laws generated by Caputo fractional differential equations of order $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ rather than memoryless exponential decay. In practical environmental monitoring, however, direct chemical quantification of $\tilde{P}(t)$ across time is prohibitively expensive; field biologists typically observe only 3 to 7 sparse, noisy measurements of microbial basal respiration $M(t)$ ($\text{CO}_2$ evolution) and extracellular enzymatic activity $E(t)$ (catalase $\text{O}_2$ displacement), confounded by erratic rainfall pulses and up to 50% missing data.

Existing computational and ecological paradigms fail to solve this inverse problem across four critical dimensions:

1. **Catastrophic Extrapolation Divergence of Polynomial KANs:** Following the introduction of Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks ([Liu et al., 2024, arXiv:2404.19756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19756)) and operator networks ([Abueidda et al., 2025, DOI: 10.1016/j.cma.2024.117699](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2024.117699)), polynomial spline and orthogonal bases (`ChebPIKAN` / `fKAN`; [Aghaei, 2024, arXiv:2406.07456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07456)) have been widely adopted for physics-informed regression. While Chebyshev polynomials fit trajectories inside the training horizon $t \in [0, 150]\text{ days}$ ($R^2 = 0.918$), their unbounded polynomial growth $\propto (t/T)^k$ causes catastrophic divergence during blind long-horizon extrapolation on $t \in (150, 365]\text{ days}$, collapsing to $R^2 = -8.132$ ($\text{RMSE} = 1.804\,\text{EC}_{50}$) on $\tilde{P}(t)$ and $R^2 = -0.909$ on soil health.
2. **Breakdown of Classical Bayesian ODE Fitting on Unmeasured States:** Classical nonlinear least-squares solvers regularized toward Soil Atlas priors (`MAP-ODE`) accurately fit observed trajectories $M(t)$ ($R^2 = 0.900$) and $E(t)$ ($R^2 = 0.891$), but fail to reconstruct the unobserved contaminant load $\tilde{P}(t)$ during extrapolation ($R^2 = -0.191 \pm 0.225$, $\text{RMSE} = 0.651\,\text{EC}_{50}$) because integer-order exponential modes cannot represent fractional subdiffusion $\alpha < 1$. Moreover, `MAP-ODE` requires iterative per-sample optimization at test time ($5.01\text{ ms/field}$).
3. **The Open Fractional Transformer–KAN Challenge (Tu et al., July 2026):** While [Yang & Wang (2025, arXiv:2409.10594)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10594) introduced the Kolmogorov-Arnold Transformer (KAT) and [Aghaei (2024, arXiv:2406.14495)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14495) proposed rational Padé KANs (`rKAN`), [Tu et al. (2026, *Fractal and Fractional*, DOI: 10.3390/fractalfract10070454)](https://doi.org/10.3390/fractalfract10070454) explicitly highlighted in July 2026 that integrating Padé rational approximants, Mittag-Leffler eigenfunctions, and fractional Abel memory kernels into attention architectures remains an unsolved fundamental challenge due to Gamma-function singularities and gradient instability. In this work, I present the first complete analytical and software realization of this architecture class.
4. **Ecological Ambiguity of Static Bioindication Indices:** The classical Integral Index of Soil Biological State (ИБСП; Kazeev, Kolesnikov, & Valkov, 2003/2016) computes a static arithmetic average of enzymatic and microbiological percentages relative to a fixed reference. As a result, static ИБСП misclassifies naturally oligotrophic sandy soils (`Arch_5`, low carrying capacity $K_m = 0.44$) as severely degraded (false positives) while producing false negatives after transient moisture pulses because it ignores both the Haldane enzyme-to-biomass hysteresis loop and the fractional memory penalty $(1-\alpha)\tilde{P}(t)$.

## 2. Mathematical Architecture (`Frac-ML-KAT-MoE`)

### 2.1. Dimensionless Fractional Biokinetic System and Boundary Identifiability

To eliminate scale unidentifiability between an unmeasured contaminant $P(t)$ and enzymatic inhibition constants, I formulate the governing Caputo fractional dynamical system of order $\alpha \in (0, 1]$ in dimensionless half-inhibition units $\tilde{P}(t) = P(t)/K_{\text{tox}}$ ($K_{\text{tox}} \equiv 1.0$, $K_i \equiv 1.0$, in units of $\text{EC}_{50}$):

$$D_t^\alpha \tilde{P}(t) = -\left(k_p + \frac{k_{\text{deg}}\Phi_{\text{env}}(t)\,M(t)}{K_s + \tilde{P}(t) + \tilde{P}(t)^2}\right)\tilde{P}(t)$$

$$D_t^\alpha M(t) = r_m \Phi_{\text{env}}(t)\,M(t)\left(1 - \frac{M(t)}{K_m}\right) - \gamma_p \frac{\tilde{P}(t)\,M(t)}{1 + \tilde{P}(t)} + \mu_{\text{sub}} \frac{\tilde{P}(t)\,E(t)}{K_s + \tilde{P}(t) + \tilde{P}(t)^2}$$

$$D_t^\alpha E(t) = \beta_E \frac{M(t)}{1 + \tilde{P}(t)^2} - \delta_E E(t)$$

where $D_t^\alpha f(t) = \frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_0^t (t-s)^{-\alpha} f'(s)\,ds$ is the Caputo derivative. Because $\tilde{P}(0)$ is never passed as an input feature, I infer the initial shock boundary estimate $\tilde{P}_{\text{boundary}}(0)$ solely from the selective quadratic suppression of extracellular enzyme activity $E(t_1)$ relative to microbial biomass $M(t_1)$ at the earliest observation $t_1 \to 0$:

$$\tilde{P}_{\text{boundary}}(0) = \sqrt{\max\!\left(0,\, \frac{E_{\text{ref}}/M_{\text{ref}}}{E(t_1)/M(t_1)} - 1\right)}, \quad \frac{E_{\text{ref}}}{M_{\text{ref}}} \approx 1.20$$

### 2.2. `Frac-KAT` Encoder: Causal Abel–Riemann–Liouville Memory Attention and Weight Tying

Let $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times N \times 5}$ denote the sparse observation tensor $[\text{pH}_{\text{norm}}, \text{NO}_{3,\text{norm}}, W_{\text{norm}}, M_{\text{obs}}, E_{\text{obs}}]$ at irregular timestamps $0 \le t_1 < t_2 < \dots < t_N \le 150\text{ days}$, paired with availability mask $\mathbf{M}_{\text{avail}} \in \{0,1\}^{B \times N \times 5}$ and rainfall flag $\mathbf{r} \in \{0,1\}^{B \times N \times 1}$. I construct a 12-dimensional input token at step $j$:

$$\mathbf{u}_j = \left[\mathbf{v}_j \odot \mathbf{m}_j + 0.35(\mathbf{v}_j - \mathbf{v}_1)\odot \mathbf{m}_j,\; \mathbf{m}_j,\; \ln(1 + t_j),\; r_j\right] \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$$

To embed non-Markovian soil memory directly into sequence aggregation, I derive causal self-attention weights modulated by the log-kernel of the Abel–Riemann–Liouville fractional integral operator $\mathcal{I}_t^\alpha$:

$$K_{\text{Abel}}(t_i, t_j; \alpha) = \frac{(t_i - t_j + \varepsilon)^{\alpha - 1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}, \quad \mathcal{A}_{ij} = \frac{\mathbf{q}_i^\top \mathbf{k}_j}{\sqrt{d_{\text{model}}}} + \gamma_h \left[(\alpha - 1)\ln(t_i - t_j + \varepsilon) - \ln\Gamma(\alpha)\right]$$

for $t_i \ge t_j$ (and $\mathcal{A}_{ij} = -\infty$ for $t_i < t_j$, preventing future information leakage). The encoder predicts both the memory exponent $\hat{\alpha} = 0.40 + 0.52\,\sigma(z_\alpha) \in [0.40, 0.92]$ and epistemic uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_\alpha = 0.01 + 0.15\,\text{softplus}(z_\sigma)$. Crucially, I enforce **Weight Tying**: the exact same scalar $\hat{\alpha}$ governs the attention kernel $K_{\text{Abel}}$, the Soil Atlas coordinate, the Padé Mittag-Leffler basis $E_{\hat{\alpha},1}(-\lambda_k t^{\hat{\alpha}})$, and the fractional health index $\text{ИБСП}_{\hat{\alpha}}(t)$.

### 2.3. `MittagLefflerPade34`: Pole-Free Global `[3/4]` Padé Approximant

Direct evaluation of the Mittag-Leffler function $E_{\alpha,1}(-z) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \frac{(-z)^k}{\Gamma(\alpha k + 1)}$ overflows for $z > 20$, whereas numerical contour integration cannot be backpropagated efficiently. I construct a differentiable `[3/4]` rational Padé approximant:

$$R_{[3/4]}^{(\alpha)}(z) = \frac{P_3(z)}{Q_4(z)} = \frac{1 + p_1 z + p_2 z^2 + p_3 z^3}{1 + q_1 z + q_2 z^2 + q_3 z^3 + q_4 z^4}$$

Let $g_k = \frac{1}{\Gamma(1 + k\alpha)}$ ($k=1,\dots,5$) and $m_k = \frac{1}{\Gamma(1 - k\alpha)} = \frac{\Gamma(k\alpha)\sin(k\pi\alpha)}{\pi}$ ($k=1,2$) via Euler's reflection formula, which analytically removes the pole of $\Gamma(1-\alpha)$ at $\alpha = 0.50$. Matching the series expansion $E_{\alpha,1}(-z) = 1 - g_1 z + g_2 z^2 - g_3 z^3 + g_4 z^4 - g_5 z^5 + \mathcal{O}(z^6)$ as $z \to 0$ and the asymptotic expansion $E_{\alpha,1}(-z) = m_1 z^{-1} - m_2 z^{-2} + \mathcal{O}(z^{-3})$ as $z \to \infty$ yields:

$$p_1 = q_1 - g_1, \qquad p_2 = q_2 - g_1 q_1 + g_2$$

and a closed-form $5\times 5$ linear system $\mathbf{A}(\alpha)\,\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}(\alpha)$ for $\mathbf{x} = [q_1, q_2, q_3, q_4, p_3]^\top$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} g_2 & -g_1 & 1 & 0 & -1 \\ -g_3 & g_2 & -g_1 & 1 & 0 \\ g_4 & -g_3 & g_2 & -g_1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & -m_1 & 1 \\ -g_1 & 1 & -m_1 & m_2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} q_1 \\ q_2 \\ q_3 \\ q_4 \\ p_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} g_3 \\ -g_4 \\ g_5 \\ 0 \\ -g_2 \end{bmatrix}$$

I solve this batched system in `float64` via `torch.linalg.solve` with analytical reverse-mode gradients $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{b}} = \mathbf{A}^{-\top}\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{x}}$ and $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{A}} = -\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{b}}\mathbf{x}^\top$. For $\alpha \in (0.90, 1.0]$, I apply a $C^1$-smooth interpolation between $R_{[3/4]}^{(0.90)}(z)$ and $\exp(-z)$. Across all $\alpha \in [0.10, 0.99]$ and $z \ge 0$, the denominator satisfies $Q_4(z) \ge 1.001 > 1$, guaranteeing strict positivity and zero real poles.

### 2.4. `PhysicsExpertBank` (Sparse Top-2 MoE) and `MultKAN` Symbolic Discovery

To discover nonlinear environmental rate modifiers without black-box opacity, I design `MultKANEnvModule` by pairing a Shelford pH bell with a two-branch log-exponential power layer:

$$\Phi_{\text{KAN}}(\mathbf{e}) = \exp\!\left(-\beta_{\text{pH}}(\text{pH} - \text{pH}_{\text{opt}})^2\right) \sum_{b=1}^2 w_b \exp\!\left(\sum_{c \in \{\text{pH}, \text{NO}_3, W\}} W_{b,c}\ln e_c\right)$$

regularized by an $L_1$ sparsity penalty $\mathcal{L}_{L1} = \|\mathbf{W}_{\text{power}}\|_1$. The contextual state $\mathbf{h}_{\text{final}}$ and dynamic drop markers $[\Phi_{\text{KAN}}, \text{pH}, \Delta E_{\text{drop}}, \Delta M_{\text{drop}}, \tilde{P}_{\text{boundary}}(0)]$ (where $\Delta E_{\text{drop}} = \min_t E_{\text{obs}} / \max_t E_{\text{obs}}$) are routed through `PhysicsExpertBank` across $K_{\text{exp}} = 4$ specialized biokinetic experts (Expert 0: Haldane inhibition; Expert 1: Monod saturation; Expert 2: Abiotic clean homeostasis; Expert 3: Acidic/osmotic stress). I select the Top-2 active mechanisms via temperature-annealed softmax gating ($\tau: 2.0 \to 0.5$) and enforce balanced utilization via the load-balancing loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{balance}} = K_{\text{exp}} \sum_{e=1}^{K_{\text{exp}}} f_e \bar{P}_e$.

### 2.5. Two-Stage Inference, Soil Atlas Passportization, and Spectral Trajectory Trunk

In Stage 1 (offline calibration), `SoilAtlasModule` maps each field onto the 5 reference Soil Atlas centroids $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 5}$ (`Arch_1` Sod-Podzolic Loam, `Arch_2` Peat-Bog, `Arch_3` Sod-Carbonate, `Arch_4` Technogenic Solonchak, `Arch_5` Sandy Forest) via inverse-variance weighted distance $d_{\text{raw}}$ and KL-divergence supervision against Dirichlet mixture weights. I define the composite anomaly metric:

$$d_{\min} = 0.25\,d_{\text{raw}} + 0.75\,\tanh\!\left(0.42\,\tilde{P}_{\text{boundary}}(0)\right)$$

and calibrate the optimal Youden threshold $d_{\min}^* = \arg\max_d (\text{TPR}_{\text{val}}(d) - \text{FPR}_{\text{val}}(d)) = 0.290$ strictly on the validation split ($N_{\text{val}} = 500$). To eliminate shrinkage bias on contaminated soils while suppressing false positives deep inside the clean zone ($d_{\min} < 0.5\,d_{\min}^*$), I apply a piecewise-smooth gate $g(d_{\min}) = 1$ for $d_{\min} \ge 0.5\,d_{\min}^*$ and $\sigma((d_{\min} - 0.35\,d_{\min}^*)/0.025)$ otherwise.

In Stage 2 (zero-shot field inference, $0.50\text{ ms/field}$), `MLKANTrunk` generates continuous trajectories over any horizon $t \in [0, 365]\text{ days}$ as a 3-mode Mittag-Leffler expansion:

$$\tilde{P}_{\text{bio}}(t) = \sum_{k=1}^3 c_{P,k}\, E_{\hat{\alpha},1}\!\left(-\lambda_k t^{\hat{\alpha}}\right), \quad M(t) = M_\infty + \sum_{k=1}^3 c_{M,k}\, E_{\hat{\alpha},1}\!\left(-\lambda_k t^{\hat{\alpha}}\right), \quad E(t) = E_\infty + \sum_{k=1}^3 c_{E,k}\, E_{\hat{\alpha},1}\!\left(-\lambda_k t^{\hat{\alpha}}\right)$$

Because $D_t^{\hat{\alpha}} E_{\hat{\alpha},1}(-\lambda_k t^{\hat{\alpha}}) = -\lambda_k E_{\hat{\alpha},1}(-\lambda_k t^{\hat{\alpha}})$ holds analytically, the Caputo physics-informed loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}$ is evaluated in closed form without finite-difference discretization error.

## 3. Numerical Verification & Empirical Benchmarks

### 3.1. Mathematical Kernel Verification (Figure 1)

Before training, I subject all mathematical primitives to strict numerical unit tests (`run_tests.py`, `unit_test_report.json`):
- **Padé `[3/4]` vs. Oberhettinger Contour Integral (Fig. 1A, 1B):** Across 300 log-spaced nodes $z \in [10^{-3}, 10^3]$, `MittagLefflerPade34` achieves maximum absolute error $\max|\Delta| = 7.67\times 10^{-7}$ at $\alpha = 0.30$, $\max|\Delta| = 3.85\times 10^{-5} < 10^{-4}$ at $\alpha = 0.50$ (verified against the exact identity $E_{0.5,1}(-z) = \text{erfcx}(z)$), and $\max|\Delta| = 1.12\times 10^{-3} < 2\times 10^{-3}$ at $\alpha = 0.78$, with minimum denominator $\min Q_4(z) = 1.0012 \ge 1.0$ and strictly positive output.
- **Autograd & Caputo ABM Solver Verification (Fig. 1C):** Double-precision `torch.autograd.gradcheck` (`eps=1e-6`, `atol=1e-4`) passes with zero `NaN`/`Inf` gradients ($\|\nabla_z \mathcal{L}\|_2 = 9.75$, $\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial \alpha = -8.31$ at $\alpha=0.50$). Against the analytical relaxation $\tilde{P}(0) E_{\alpha,1}(-k_p t^\alpha)$ at $k_{\text{deg}} = 0$, the Diethelm ABM predictor-corrector solver ([Diethelm et al., 2002](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1016592219341)) achieves relative error $0.00331\% < 0.5\%$.

### 3.2. Blind Long-Horizon Extrapolation $150 \to 365$ Days (Figure 2)

I generate a stratified 4,000-scenario Soil Atlas dataset (20% per archetype across `Train = 3000`, `Val = 500`, `Test = 500`) with only $6\text{--}8$ noisy observations ($\sigma_{\text{noise}} = 25\%$, 50% missingness) restricted to $t \in [0, 150]\text{ days}$, and evaluate blind extrapolation on $t \in (150, 365]\text{ days}$ with 1000-fold bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (Fig. 2A–2D):
- On measured microbial biomass $M(t)$ and enzyme activity $E(t)$, classical `MAP-ODE` ($R^2_M = 0.900$, $R^2_E = 0.891$) is competitive with `TerraKAN` ($R^2_M = 0.894 \pm 0.015$, $R^2_E = 0.910 \pm 0.013$).
- However, on the **unmeasured contaminant load $\tilde{P}(t)$**, `MAP-ODE` collapses to $R^2 = -0.191 \pm 0.225$ ($\text{RMSE} = 0.651\,\text{EC}_{50}$) and `ChebPIKAN` diverges to $R^2 = -8.132 \pm 3.014$ ($\text{RMSE} = 1.804\,\text{EC}_{50}$), whereas **`TerraKAN` achieves $R^2 = 0.908 \pm 0.019$ ($\text{RMSE} = 0.181\,\text{EC}_{50}$)** with near-zero mean bias error ($\text{MBE} = +0.0079\,\text{EC}_{50}$).
- On the dynamic health index $\text{ИБСП}_\alpha(t)$, `TerraKAN` achieves $R^2 = 0.886 \pm 0.016$ ($\text{RMSE} = 6.88\%$), outperforming `MAP-ODE` ($R^2 = 0.769$, $\text{RMSE} = 9.81\%$) and `Simple-ML-MLP` ($R^2 = 0.742$, $\text{RMSE} = 10.37\%$) while running in $0.50\text{ ms/field}$ vs. $5.01\text{ ms/field}$ for `MAP-ODE`.

### 3.3. Haldane Hysteresis Loop & False-Negative Safety Audit (Figure 3)

As shown in Fig. 3A, `TerraKAN` reconstructs the non-monotonic Haldane hysteresis loop in phase space $(\tilde{P}, M, E)$ (`R^2_P = 0.908`). On clean nutrient-poor sandy forest soil (`Arch_5`, $K_m = 0.44$, Fig. 3B), where static ИБСП drops below 50%, $\text{ИБСП}_\alpha(t)$ remains within $94\text{--}99\%$ because it normalizes biomass against the inferred asymptotic capacity $M_\infty$. Furthermore, auditing the $17.4\%$ borderline low-dose contaminated scenarios missed by the binary anomaly detector ($d_{\min} < d_{\min}^*$, False Negatives) reveals that their initial enzyme/biomass dip holds $\text{ИБСП}_\alpha(t)$ in the yellow warning zone (mean minimum $72.2\% < 75\%$ in $70.6\%$ of cases), with a median extrapolation error of only $0.76\%$ (overall test median error $3.2\%$ vs. $13.8\%$ for `ChebPIKAN`, Fig. 3C). Sensitivity analysis across $\lambda_{\text{mem}} \in [0.15, 0.45]$ confirms stable $R^2 \in [0.878, 0.891]$.

### 3.4. Soil Atlas Passportization & Multi-Seed Detector Audit (Figure 4)

On the 500-field test set, `SoilAtlasModule` achieves **Macro-Accuracy $= 88.4\%$** and **Macro-F1 $= 0.890$** across the 5 soil archetypes (Fig. 4A), while `Frac-KAT` recovers the true fractional order $\alpha_{\text{true}}$ with **$R^2 = 0.9248$** ($\text{RMSE} = 0.0240$, Fig. 4B). With the threshold frozen on `Val (500)` at $d_{\min}^* = 0.290$ ($\text{Val TPR} = 84.6\%$, $\text{FPR} = 3.6\%$, Youden $J = 0.810$), `Test (500)` achieves $\text{TPR} = 82.6\%$, $\text{FPR} = 4.5\%$, $J = 0.780$ ($|\Delta\text{TPR}| = 2.05\%$, Fig. 4C). Across 5 independent stratified `Val/Test` splits (`seeds = [42, 7, 13, 99, 2026]`), the mean gap is $|\Delta\text{TPR}| = 1.85\% \pm 0.95\% < 4.0\%$.

### 3.5. Neuro-Symbolic Discovery & Exact Tensor Unpacking (Figure 5)

In Fig. 5A, `MultKAN` accurately recovers the underlying environmental power laws from 25% noisy observations: nitrate exponent $0.345$ vs. true $0.350$, moisture exponent $0.394$ vs. true $0.400$, negligible pH power weight $0.000$ vs. $0.000$, and Shelford pH Gaussian $\exp(-2.36(\text{pH}-0.66)^2)$ vs. true $\exp(-2.50(\text{pH}-0.65)^2)$. In Fig. 5B, `PhysicsExpertBank` routes clean soils to Expert 2 ($56\%$), moderate doses to Expert 1 ($49\%$), high-load shocks to Expert 0 (Haldane, $54\%$), and acidic peat soils to Expert 3 ($52\%$). In Fig. 5C, `unpack_symbolic()` exports human-readable closed-form equations whose exact `float64` evaluation matches the neural tensor output within $\max|\Delta_{\text{exact}}| = 9.96\times 10^{-13} < 10^{-6}$ ($2.45\times 10^{-3} < 5\times 10^{-3}$ after 3-digit string rounding).

### 3.6. Model Misspecification ("Alien Physics"), Noise Robustness & 3-Seed Ablation (Figure 6)

To prove that `TerraKAN` does not merely memorize its own generator, I evaluate zero-shot generalization on two out-of-distribution benchmarks without retraining (`fig6_ablation_and_noise_robustness.png`, Panel B):
1. **Two-Compartment Sorption ODE + Kohlrausch–Weibull Stretched Exponential:** `TerraKAN` achieves $R^2 = 0.847$ ($\text{RMSE} = 4.14\%$), outperforming `MAP-ODE` ($R^2 = 0.644$, $\text{RMSE} = 6.30\%$) and wide-prior `MAP-ODE` ($R^2 = 0.558$, $\text{RMSE} = 7.02\%$).
2. **Out-of-Atlas Fractional Order $\alpha \in [0.35, 0.45] \cup [0.91, 0.96]$:** `TerraKAN` achieves $R^2 = 0.905$ ($\text{RMSE} = 8.11\%$) vs. `MAP-ODE` ($R^2 = 0.752$, $\text{RMSE} = 13.08\%$).

Under multiplicative observation noise up to $40\%$ (Fig. 6A), `TerraKAN` retains $R^2 = 0.788$ vs. $0.415$ for `MAP-ODE`. Across 3 independent training seeds (`42, 101, 777`, Fig. 6C), removing `MoE` drops $R^2$ from $0.887 \pm 0.007$ to $0.834 \pm 0.009$, removing `MultKAN` drops $R^2$ to $0.819 \pm 0.009$, removing `Soil Atlas` drops $R^2$ to $0.798 \pm 0.009$, removing $\alpha$ weight tying drops $R^2$ to $0.781 \pm 0.011$, and replacing Abel fractional attention with a standard MLP drops $R^2$ to $0.743 \pm 0.015$.

### 3.7. Microcosm Dry-Run Validation on 36 Vessels (Figure 7)

Finally, I validate the frozen checkpoint `terrakan_v4_frozen.pt` (`model.eval()`, `torch.no_grad()`) on a 36-vessel laboratory microcosm protocol (`process_buckets_csv.py`, `fig_dry_run_36pots.png`) spanning 3 soil series (`S1_sand`, `S2_loam`, `S3_peat`) with observations on days `[0, 3, 7, 14, 21, 28]`, vessel-specific missing respiration measurements, and blind forecasting to day 60. Normalizing catalase $\text{O}_2$ (proxy for $E$) and basal respiration $\text{CO}_2$ (proxy for $M$) strictly against the day-0 control mean of the **same `soil_series`** and computing diagnoses solely from model outputs without access to `is_control_G0`, all 6 `G0` controls achieve $\tilde{P}_{\text{bio}}(0) \le 0.001 < 0.5\,\text{EC}_{50}$ and $\text{ИБСП}(0) \in [92.1\%, 99.5\%] > 92\%$, all 8 clean nutrient-depleted `G1` vessels are correctly separated as poor/depleted soil ($\max \tilde{P}_{\text{bio}}(0) = 0.016 < 1.0$, $\text{ИБСП}(0) \in [64.8\%, 68.5\%] < 85\%$, $\max d_{\min} = 0.148 \le 0.290$), and all 12 heavily loaded `G3` vessels trigger the anomaly detector ($\min d_{\min} = 0.593 > 0.290$, $\tilde{P}_{\text{bio}}(0) \in [2.46, 3.11]\,\text{EC}_{50}$).
